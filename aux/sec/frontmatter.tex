\begin{abstract}
This paper extends the optimal time-step selection with non-iterative defect
correction (OTS-NIDC) framework of Chu (2011) from deterministic reaction--diffusion
equations to the \emph{moment hierarchy} of stochastic reaction--diffusion equations
with linear multiplicative noise. Because It\^o's formula closes the hierarchy exactly
for affine drift with linear multiplicative noise, every moment equation is a
deterministic reaction--diffusion PDE to which OTS-NIDC applies verbatim: a single
optimal step $\dto = \dx^2/(6D)$, independent of both the moment order and the noise
amplitude, cancels the leading truncation term, and an explicit correction restores
fourth-order global accuracy on a three-point stencil. We derive the scalar family,
the coupled mean-field/second-moment systems, and the competing-time-step problem
that arises between them, with resolution via decoupled blocks and multirate
subcycling with an exact mismatch correction. All claims are entered in a
\emph{status ledger}: verified numerically (V), proven in sketch (P), stable-region
supported (S), open (O), or contradicted by our own verification harness (C) --- most
notably, the coupled system currently measures at second order, not the asserted
fourth, and we report this rather than bury it. The final section maps the road
out of the linear world: Cole--Hopf makes the stochastic Burgers equation a
multiplicative-noise stochastic heat equation for $\phi$, so weakly convergent
schemes for $\phi$ moments transfer to exponential functionals of Burgers ---
the log-normal observables that fail Gaussian closure --- while the KPZ regime
$u = \log \phi$ retains well-defined roughness observables in the same window
where the $\phi$-moments themselves diverge. We frame what is proved, what is
plausible, and what must be rebuilt, and invite correction.
\end{abstract}

\section*{How to read this paper (the living-paper contract)}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{How to read this paper (the living-paper contract)}

This is a \emph{living paper}: a submission-track manuscript that carries its own
epistemic state. It will be re-released as derivations firm up, verification
harnesses expand, and open items resolve. Two mechanisms enforce that:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Status ledger.} Every substantive mathematical or numerical claim in
the text carries one of five tags, with the tag defined by a reproducible artifact
in the repository, not by confidence:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Tag & Meaning \& evidence required \\
\midrule
\tagV & \textbf{Verified.} A convergence/stability number produced by a script in
\texttt{code/} against an independent exact or high-resolution reference.\\
\tagP & \textbf{Proven (sketch).} A complete argument the reader can check line by
line; no numerical artifact, but no unproved lemma either.\\
\tagS & \textbf{Supported.} Backed by an analysis artifact whose conclusion is
weaker than the claim (e.g.\ scalar von Neumann used for a coupled statement).\\
\tagO & \textbf{Open.} Stated as a target or conjecture; no artifact yet.\\
\tagC & \textbf{Contradicted.} Our own harness produces numbers inconsistent with
the claim. Kept in the text, with the discrepancy flagged.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\item \textbf{Change log.} Every release records what moved between states.
\end{enumerate}
