% Section 6: The weird stuff — Cole-Hopf, KPZ
\section{The nonlinear frontier: Cole--Hopf, stochastic Burgers, and KPZ}
\label{sec:weird}

Everything above lives inside the closure-exact class: linear reaction, linear
(or additive) multiplicative noise. This section maps the road out --- and is
deliberately the part of the paper where the ledger does the most work, because
the beautiful connection here is also the one where the current framework
\emph{cannot follow}.

\subsection{Stochastic Burgers is a stochastic heat equation in disguise}

The forced stochastic Burgers equation (in 1D, written for the slope $u$) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:burgers}
\partial_t u + u\,\partial_x u = \nu\,\partial_{xx}u \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
u = -2\nu\,\partial_x\log\phi .
\end{equation}
Under Cole--Hopf, $\phi$ solves a stochastic heat equation with
\emph{multiplicative} noise. In its simplest well-posed version (It\^o, with the
noise entering as $\sigma\,\phi\,\xi$ plus the It\^o correction absorbed into
the equation for $\phi$), $\phi$ is \emph{exactly} the linear-multiplicative-
noise model \eqref{eq:spde} --- the protagonist of \S\ref{sec:stochastic}:

\begin{theorem}[Cole--Hopf transfer of weak schemes]\label{thm:hopf}
Let $\phi$ solve the linear multiplicative-noise stochastic heat equation, so
its moments $M_k^\phi = \E[\phi^k]$ satisfy the closed family \eqref{eq:moments}
and are computable by OTS-NIDC to fourth order (Table~\ref{tab:scalar}).
Then every moment of the exponential functional $\phi^k(x,t)$ of the Burgers
velocity field --- in particular the $k$-th moments of
$u(x,t) = -2\nu\,\partial_x\log\phi(x,t)$ that are expressible as
derivatives of $\log M_k^\phi$ or of ratios of $\phi$-moments --- inherits that
accuracy, because $u$ is a fixed smooth functional of the moments and the
scheme converges in exactly the norm that functional reads.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Mild solutions: $\phi(t) = S_t\phi_0\cdot\mathcal{E}_t$ with
$\mathcal{E}_t = \exp(\sigma W_t - \frac12\sigma^2 t\,\cdot)$ the stochastic
exponential (a strictly positive martingale field); hence $\phi>0$ and
$\log\phi$ is smooth wherever $\phi$ is. Moments of $\phi$ are the closed
hierarchy; derivatives of $\log$-moments are smooth functions of those moments,
so a solver converging at order $q$ in the moments yields order-$q$ accuracy in
any fixed derivative of their logarithms. The Cole--Hopf identity converts
those to moments of Burgers observables.
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Status:} \tagP. Why this matters: it moves \emph{two} canonical
nonlinear objects --- Burgers velocity statistics and log-normal-type fields
--- inside the reach of a linear theory, without any moment truncation. Note
the honest fine print: $\E[u]$ and higher $u$-moments involve
$\E[\partial_x\phi/\phi]$, a \emph{ratio} of moments, not a moment --- the
transfer in Theorem~\ref{thm:hopf} is clean for quantities that are smooth
functionals of the $M_k^\phi$ (e.g.\ $\partial_x \log \E[\phi^k]$, structure
functions built from derivatives of log-moments), and requires ratio-variance
care otherwise. Making this precise and tested is open (item \ref{open:hopf}).

\subsection{The KPZ window: what stays finite when the moments leave}

The Kardar--Parisi--Zhang equation is the log of the same field:
$u = \log\phi$ turns the multiplicative-noise heat equation into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kpz}
\partial_t u = \nu\,\partial_{xx}u + \tfrac{\lambda}2(\partial_x u)^2 + \eta ,
\end{equation}
with $\eta$ effective white noise and the It\^o drift folded into the
nonlinearity. Here the relationship between finiteness and observability
\emph{inverts}, and this inversion is the section's central observation.

\begin{proposition}[KPZ window]\label{prop:kpz}
In 1D, KPZ itself is well-posed (the Cole--Hopf solution is the definition of
its solution), and its natural observables --- the height field $u$, its
roughness $\mathrm{Var}[u]$, spatial correlations of $\log\phi$ --- remain
finite and well-defined for all time. It is the raw moments of $\phi$,
$M_k^\phi=\E[\phi^k]$, that diverge (log-normal tails, $M_k^\phi \sim
e^{\frac12k^2\sigma^2 t\cdot}$): beyond a time of order
$t_\ast \sim \log K / \sigma^2$ even the $K$-th moment overflows any fixed
precision. The divergence is a property of the \emph{observable} $M_k^\phi$,
not of the dynamics.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\phi$ is the stochastic exponential: $\phi = \exp(\sigma W_t -
\frac12\sigma^2t)$ applied to the diffused initial data, so
$\log\phi$ is Gaussian at each point with variance $\sigma^2 t$ (per mode, with
diffusive decorrelation). $\E[\phi^k]$ then carries the
$\exp(\frac12 k^2\sigma^2t)$ log-normal factor --- finite for each fixed
$t,k$, but overflowing IEEE double precision ($\sim e^{709}$) at $t \approx
1418/(k^2\sigma^2)$, i.e.\ at practical times for even moderate $k\sigma$.
Meanwhile $u=\log\phi$ has bounded Gaussian moments for all $t$.
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Status:} \tagS (literature + representation argument; no
numerics in this repo). The practical reading for this paper:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Work in $u$-space observables.} Roughness, correlation functions
of $u$, and $\log$-moment derivatives stay in the solver's finite window
indefinitely; the recommended solver target for KPZ-adjacent problems is the
$\log$-moment hierarchy, not the raw hierarchy.
\item \textbf{But the closure is gone.} The equation \eqref{eq:kpz} for $u$ is
\emph{nonlinear in the field}, so the moment hierarchy of $u$ does not close:
$\E[(\partial_xu)^2]$ is a second moment feeding the mean equation, and the
tower reopens. The exact-closure theorem (Prop.~\ref{prop:closure}) does not
reach KPZ; Gaussian closure fails precisely here (log-normal intermittency is
the textbook failure mode). Nothing in \S\ref{sec:method} applies to
\eqref{eq:kpz} directly.
\item \textbf{The window is nonetheless real.} What the theory \emph{can}
deliver inside the window: (i) $\phi$-moments below the overflow time ---
$K$ moments to time $t \sim \log K$, so a tower of solves extends the window
logarithmically; (ii) all $u$-space statistics of the \emph{linear} problem
(no KPZ nonlinearity), exactly; (iii) the Cole--Hopf bridge
(Thm.~\ref{thm:hopf}) for the observables it covers. What it cannot yet
deliver: direct high-order weak integration of the KPZ nonlinearity
\eqref{eq:kpz} itself \tagO --- the honest frontier.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why this section is in the paper}

A living paper should show its edges. The Cole--Hopf/KPZ connection is the
most-cited potential application of moment-level solvers (turbulence
analogues, directed polymers, growth interfaces), and it is also the setting
where the paper's core machinery is \emph{not yet} valid beyond the linear
window. Rather than claim the frontier or quietly omit it, we state the
transfer theorem that does hold (\tagP), the window proposition that scopes
it (\tagS), and the open problem that gates the rest (item \ref{open:kpz}):
\emph{a closure scheme for the $(\partial_xu)^2$ moment tower that is exact in
the same sense Prop.~\ref{prop:closure} is exact for linear noise --- or a
proof that none exists.}
