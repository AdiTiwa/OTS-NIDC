% Section 4: Coupled reaction-diffusion systems
\section{The method II: coupled reaction--diffusion systems}\label{sec:coupled}

We now couple species --- the deterministic core needed for the stochastic
two-block system of \S\ref{sec:stochastic}, and the natural home of the paper's
hardest verification question.

\subsection{Mean-field and second-moment blocks}

For $N$ species with diagonal diffusion, linear reaction coupling, and (here)
zero noise --- the deterministic skeleton of the moment systems to come ---
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupledrd}
\partial_t u^{(i)} = D_i\,\partial_{xx}u^{(i)} + \sum_j R_{ij}u^{(j)}.
\end{equation}
Taking $u^{(i)}=\E[\cdot]$ of a stochastic system instead produces the
\emph{mean-field (M) block} of \S\ref{sec:stochastic}; the pairwise products
produce the \emph{second-moment (V) block} with diffusion coefficients
$D_{ij}=D_i+D_j$. Both have the identical structure of \eqref{eq:coupledrd}
with different $D$'s and source terms, so the analysis of this section applies
to each block verbatim.

The per-equation LTE analysis of \S\ref{sec:method} goes through with
$D\to D_i$ (M block) or $D\to D_{ij}$ (V block), the reaction coupling entering
only through the analogues of Groups 2--3. The optimal steps are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupledopt}
\dto^{(i)} = \frac{\dx^2}{6D_i} \quad(\text{M, species }i),
\qquad
\dto^{(ij)} = \frac{\dx^2}{6(D_i+D_j)} \quad(\text{V, pair }i,j),
\end{equation}
with first-level corrections $C^{(1),(i)}$ and $C^{(1),(ij)}$ of the same
algebraic form as \eqref{eq:C1}, derived term-by-term in \texttt{OTS-NIDC.md}
\S8--9. In the moment hierarchy $D_i = D$ these become $\dx^2/6D$ and
$\dx^2/12D$ --- a strict \textbf{2:1 ratio}, independently verified in
\texttt{notes/Di\_eq\_D\_verification.md}: measured $\dt_M/\dt_V = 2.0000$ at
$N=32,64,128$ \tagV.

\subsection{The M/V decoupling}

\begin{proposition}[Exact block decoupling]\label{prop:decouple}
For affine drift with linear multiplicative noise (the class of
\S\ref{sec:stochastic}), the M-system is closed in $\{m_i\}$ and the
V-system is closed in $\{V_{ij}\}$: neither block feeds the other, for any
$D_i$, $R_{ij}$, $\sigma_{ik}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
In the M-block, every stochastic contribution is $\E[\,\mathrm{d}W_k\,]=0$: no
second-moment term can appear. In the V-block, It\^o's product rule contributes
the quadratic covariation $\sum_k \sigma_{ik}\sigma_{jk}u_k^2\,\mathrm{d}t$,
whose expectation is a sum of second moments $V_{kk}$ --- the drift being affine
and the noise being linear, no third moments are generated. Closure is a
structural consequence of (affine drift) $\times$ (linear noise); it is exactly
what fails for square-root (chemical Langevin) noise, whose hierarchy closes at
no finite order \citep{jovanovski2025}.
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Status:} \tagP. Consequence: the two blocks are two independent
programs --- no communication, no interpolation, no synchronization constraint
\emph{between} blocks, for any diffusivities. The residual problem is
\emph{within}-block: species $i$ and $j$ with $D_i \ne D_j$ want different
$\dto$'s.

\subsection{Competing optimal steps within a block}

The two failure modes of a naive single step frame the problem.

\paragraph{Failure 1: shared step without correction.} If species 1 (fast,
$D_1$) is forced onto species 2's optimum $\dt^\ast=\tau_2$, its Group-1
cancellation breaks. The one-step identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mismatchid}
e(\dt^\ast) \;=\; \dt^\ast D_1\,\tilde u_{xxxx}
\Bigl[\tfrac{\dt^\ast D_1}{2} - \tfrac{\dx^2}{12}\Bigr] + O((\dt^\ast)^3)
\end{equation}
is exact for every $\dt^\ast$, and writing $\dt^\ast = \tau_1 + \epsilon$ turns
the bracket into $\epsilon D_1/2$ \emph{identically} (no Taylor truncation in
$\epsilon$). Hence:

\begin{theorem}[Exact mismatch correction]\label{thm:mismatch}
For any shared step $\dt^\ast = \tau_1 + \epsilon > 0$ that is stable for
species 1, subtracting \eqref{eq:mismatchid} (with $\tilde u_{xxxx}$ from a
5-point stencil) restores species 1's full accuracy. The correction is
algebraic, not asymptotic, in $\epsilon$ --- including $\epsilon$ of the same
size as $\tau_1$ itself.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Status:} \tagV (worked example $D_1=3, D_2=1$ at $r=3$,
\texttt{examples/test\_regime1\_D1\_3\_D2\_1.py}; correction there is
grid-independent --- the $\dx^4$ cancels, leaving pure stencil weights).

\paragraph{Failure 2: naive splitting.} Lie--Trotter splitting with the
coupling frozen over a macro step $H$ incurs a sustained $O(H)$ forcing error;
accumulated over $T/H$ cycles this is a global $O(H) = O(\dx^2)$ error ---
worse than the uncorrected base scheme. Freezing is not viable; coupling data
data must be interpolated (order $m \ge p$ by the Gear--Wells bookkeeping
(see Thm.~\ref{thm:remainder}'s setting), cubic Hermite sufficing through
$p=2$ \citep{gear1984,fok2010}).

\begin{theorem}[Regime boundary]\label{thm:regime}
Forcing the fast species onto the slow species' optimal step
$\dt^\ast=\tau_2=\dx^2/6D_2$ is stable for the fast species iff
\[
r := D_1/D_2 \;\le\; 3 .
\]
For $r>3$ the shared step is unconditionally unstable and subcycling is
mandatory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
FTCS stability needs $\dt^\ast \le \dx^2/2D_1$; substituting
$\dt^\ast = \dx^2/6D_2$ gives $r\le3$. The threshold is grid-independent
because both sides scale as $\dx^2$.
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Status:} \tagS --- derived from plain FTCS; the corrected
scheme's stability region (Prop.~\ref{prop:stability} is scalar-only) has not
been re-derived, so the boundary is supported, not proven, for the corrected
coupled scheme (open item \ref{open:stab}).

\begin{theorem}[Remainder-step uniformity]\label{thm:remainder}
Under subcycling with macro step $H=\tau_2$, $n=\lfloor r\rfloor$ exact
substeps of $\tau_1$, and one remainder substep $\rho = \tau_1\,
\mathrm{frac}(r)$: the remainder's leading error
$e(\rho) = -\tfrac{D_1^2}2\,\rho(\tau_1-\rho)\,\tilde u_{xxxx}+\cdots$
vanishes at both $\mathrm{frac}(r)\to0$ and $\to1$, and obeys the uniform bound
$|e(\rho)| \le \tfrac{D_1^2\tau_1^2}8 |\tilde u_{xxxx}| = O(\dx^4)$ for all
$\mathrm{frac}(r)\in[0,1)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\rho(\tau_1-\rho)$ is a downward parabola on $[0,\tau_1]$, maximal at the
midpoint, zero at both ends. No value of the step ratio is singular; the
discriminator is how \emph{often} a mismatched step is taken (once per cycle
here), not how close $r$ is to an integer.
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Status:} \tagP for the leading (Group-1) term --- explicitly a
leading-only result; the Group-2/3 bookkeeping at arbitrary $\rho$ is open
(item \ref{open:higher}). Verified end-to-end at the integer ratio $r=4$
($\rho=0$, four exact substeps) in \texttt{examples/test\_regime2\_D1\_1\_D2\_4.py}
\tagV for the regime-2 algorithm as a whole.

\subsection{The coupled contradiction: the ledger in action}

\begin{corollary}[Under separate stepping; \textbf{contradicted}]\label{cor:coupled}
\emph{Claim as originally drafted:} evolving the M-block at $\dto^{(i)}$ and the
V-block at $\dto^{(ij)}$ (2:1 in the hierarchy) preserves fourth-order accuracy
in both blocks with three-point stencils.
\end{corollary}

This claim is tagged \tagC. The harness \texttt{coupled\_verify.py}
(\texttt{code/verify/21ratio/}) implements the $\S8.9/\S9.8$ corrections
faithfully against a validated high-resolution reference and measures
\textbf{order $\approx 1.99$ (M) and $2.02$ (V)} --- second order, not fourth ---
while confirming (i) the 2:1 ratio exactly, and (ii) that the corrections
reduce error constants by $3$--$40\times$ over plain forward Euler, so the
corrections are directionally right but an $O(\dx^2)$ defect survives. Two
hypotheses, not yet distinguished:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)] \textbf{Derivation gap:} the coupled corrections miss a cross-term
(e.g.\ the discrete $\ddx$ acting on a \emph{different} coupled field inside
$C^{(1)}$ injects an $O(\dx^2)$ error that caps the scheme, unlike the scalar
case where the correction acts on its own field), or the synchronization of
clocks leaves a residual.
\item[(B)] \textbf{Harness bug} in exactly the coupled-field plumbing.
\end{enumerate}
We report both because the ledger does not allow burying either: until this is
resolved, the coupled fourth-order claim is \emph{withdrawn from} the paper's
proven core, and the honest statement is: \emph{scalar fourth order is verified;
coupled fourth order is open, with a reproducible contradiction and a
candidate mechanism.} The discrepancy gates submission (\S\ref{sec:open}).
